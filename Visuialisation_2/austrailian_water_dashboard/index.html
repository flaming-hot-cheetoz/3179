THE STORY IN THREE STEPS

From a national high to local differences

01 / DEMAND

Use has rebounded

Australia used 17,223 GL in 2023–24—the highest value in this ten-year series. That is a 12.9% rise on 2022–23 and 7.3% above 2014–15.

02 / SECTORS

Agriculture leads

Agriculture accounted for about 73% of the water distributed to the listed sectors in 2023–24. Distributed use is not the same measure as total water consumption.

03 / HOUSEHOLDS

State averages differ

Average household use ranged from 155 kL in Tasmania to 330 kL in the Northern Territory. These are state averages—not a prediction for every household.

About this story: this is an Australian case study, not a comparison between countries. One kilolitre (kL) equals 1,000 litres; one gigalitre (GL) equals one billion litres. Hover over a chart for a quick value, or select a mark to pin its details below.

Who uses distributed water?

Annual distributed water use by sector, GL

Agriculture received 7,618 GL of distributed water in 2023–24—about 73% of the listed sector total. From 2022–23, the ABS replaced survey-based irrigation estimates with satellite-derived estimates; read the recent agricultural trend with care, particularly for Queensland, South Australia and Tasmania.

WHY THESE CHARTS?

A visual guide to the measures

Why: Help a general reader understand how Australian water use has changed, which sectors account for distributed supply, and how household averages differ across states.

What: ABS Water Account series supplied in the CSV, paired with state boundary geometry. The CSV’s older-edition records are excluded where its notes warn that methods are not comparable.

How: Trend and sector charts establish the story; a heatmap, scatterplot and three distinct maps support different comparisons. Hover tooltips reveal exact figures without turning the page into an analysis tool.

“Distributed water use” is water supplied through distribution systems and is not interchangeable with “total water consumption.” Household use excludes self-extracted water. Read each chart’s unit and note before comparing measures.

Data sources: Australian Bureau of Statistics (ABS), Water Account, Australia 2023–24 (released 11 December 2025; the latest account included in the supplied CSV); and ABS ASGS 2021 state/territory boundaries, distributed by GeoBoundaries (dataset AUS-ADM1-82085004, CC BY 4.0). The geometry is simplified for faster loading; the “Other Territories” feature is omitted because the CSV has no matching state measure. ABS measures are joined to boundaries by state name. The small map-value CSV is derived from the supplied ABS observations and adds map coordinates/layout positions.

Map projections and idioms: the two geographic maps use Mercator projection; the third is a tile cartogram with equal-sized state tiles. Boundary geometry provides context, not an area-based statistical measure.
